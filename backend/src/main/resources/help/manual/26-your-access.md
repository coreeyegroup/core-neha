# Your access: what you can do and why
> Why you see some menus and not others, how roles, positions and scope work, and what to do when something is refused.
<!-- audience: everyone -->

## See your own access

Open the **[Access guide](/help/access)** (Help → Access guide). It is built from the system itself and shows:

- your roles and where they apply (a centre, a region, or the whole organisation);
- every action you can do, grouped by area, with the role that gives it to you;
- the menus you can open;
- the actions your organisation uses that are not in your roles.

## Your position does not give access

Your **position** is your job title, for example *Pharmacist* or *Receptionist*. It records where you work. It does not open any screen by itself.

Access comes from a **role** that your administrators assign to your user account. A role is a list of permissions, and it applies at a **scope**:

| Scope | Where the role works |
|---|---|
| Centre | That centre only |
| Region (state, city or area) | Every centre in that part of the organisation |
| Whole organisation | Every centre, and organisation-wide tasks such as the item master |

If you work at two centres, you need a role at each centre, or one role for the region that covers both.

## Why a menu or a button is missing

A menu appears when you hold at least one of the permissions it needs. Inside a screen, each button checks its own permission at the centre of the record. Something can be missing because:

- your roles do not include that permission;
- your role covers another centre, not this one;
- your organisation's licence does not include that module (for example, laboratory);
- your role has ended (roles can have an end date);
- the work needs a **second person**: for example, you cannot approve a discount you requested, validate lab results you entered, or pass the quality check on spectacles you fitted.

## When an action is refused

The message shows a short code and a request id. Every refusal is recorded in the audit trail with the missing permission. If your work needs that action, ask your administrator to assign a role that includes it. Two administrators handle every access change: one requests, a different one approves.
