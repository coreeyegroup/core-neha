Cataract surgery

A cataract is a clouding of the eye's natural lens, most often with age. Vision becomes dim or hazy, colours fade, and lights cause glare, especially at night.

  • Assessment: vision, a slit-lamp examination of the lens, and optical biometry to calculate the power of the lens implant.
  • Surgery: stitchless, blade-free micro-incision phacoemulsification as day-care surgery. The cloudy lens is replaced with a clear intraocular lens.
  • Lens choices: monofocal lenses for clear distance vision, toric lenses that also correct astigmatism, and multifocal lenses for distance and near, from Zeiss and Alcon. The counsellor explains each option and its cost.
  • Recovery: eye drops for a few weeks, a review the next day, and new glasses once the eye has settled.

LASIK and refractive surgery

Laser vision correction reshapes the cornea to correct short sight, long sight and astigmatism, for adults whose glasses power has been stable.

  • Suitability tests: corneal topography, corneal thickness (pachymetry) and a dilated retina examination.
  • Contact lens wearers: stop wearing them for the period advised before the tests.
  • Procedure: a few minutes for each eye under numbing drops; vision usually improves within a day or two.

At a glance

  • Corneal topography
  • Pachymetry
  • Dilated retina check
  • Same-day procedure

Retina and diabetic eye care

The retina is the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye. Many of its diseases need prompt, specialist care.

  • Diabetic retinopathy: diabetes can damage the retina's blood vessels without symptoms. Everyone with diabetes should have a dilated retina examination every year.
  • Macular degeneration: a blurred or distorted patch in the centre of vision, usually after 50.
  • Retinal tears and detachment: new floaters, flashes of light or a curtain over vision need to be examined the same day.
  • Diagnostics and treatment: OCT scans, fundus fluorescein angiography, retinal laser and vitreo-retinal surgery.

At a glance

  • HD OCT
  • Fundus fluorescein angiography
  • Retinal laser
  • Vitreo-retinal surgery

Glaucoma

Glaucoma damages the optic nerve, usually because the pressure inside the eye is too high. Side vision is lost first, slowly and painlessly. Lost vision cannot be restored, but further loss can usually be prevented.

  • Who should be checked: everyone over 40, and earlier with a family history of glaucoma, diabetes, high myopia or long-term steroid use.
  • Tests: eye pressure, examination of the optic nerve, automated visual fields and OCT.
  • Treatment: daily eye drops for most people, laser or surgery when needed, and regular reviews for life.

At a glance

  • Non-contact tonometry
  • Humphrey visual fields
  • Optic nerve OCT
  • Lifelong follow-up

Children's eye care and squint

Children rarely complain about their eyesight. Early examination protects their vision for life.

  • Bring your child if they hold books close, squint or tilt their head, have a turning eye, rub their eyes often, or have headaches after school or screen time.
  • We assess refractive errors, lazy eye (amblyopia), squint and digital eye strain.
  • Treatment includes glasses, patching and exercises for amblyopia, and squint correction when needed.

At a glance

  • Vision screening
  • Amblyopia therapy
  • Squint evaluation
  • Spectacles for children

Cornea, dry eye and eye examinations

A complete eye examination checks your vision, glasses power, eye pressure, and the health of the front and back of the eye.

  • Dry eye: tear-film assessment and treatment for gritty, burning or watering eyes.
  • Cornea: pterygium management and treatment of corneal infections.
  • Spectacles: your prescription after the doctor's examination, with in-house optical dispensing.

  • Pterygium care
  • In-house optical

Technology and facilities

Diagnostics for accurate staging and surgical planning, and operation theatres run to NABH guidelines.

  • Micro-incision phacoemulsificationCataract removal through an incision of about 2.2 mm, for a quicker recovery.
  • High-definition OCTCross-sectional scans of the retina, macula and optic nerve.
  • Fundus fluorescein angiographyMaps blood flow in the retina, and leakage from diabetic eye disease.
  • Automated visual fieldsHumphrey field analysis to detect early loss of side vision in glaucoma.
  • Tonometry and pachymetryEye pressure and corneal thickness, measured without touching the eye.
  • Modular operation theatresSterile suites with HEPA filtration, operating microscopes and autoclave sterilisation.
