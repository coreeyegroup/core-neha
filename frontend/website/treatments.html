Cataract

The lens inside the eye slowly becomes cloudy, most often with age. Vision becomes dim, colours fade, and lights cause glare, especially at night. Glasses help only for a while.

  • Diagnosis: vision and glasses power, an examination of the lens with a slit lamp, and measurements of the eye (biometry) to choose the lens implant.
  • Treatment: surgery replaces the cloudy lens with a clear artificial lens (IOL). Phacoemulsification uses a small incision; most patients go home the same day.
  • Lens choices: monofocal lenses for clear distance vision, toric lenses that also correct astigmatism, and other premium options. The counsellor explains the choices and costs.
  • Afterwards: eye drops for a few weeks, a review the next day, and new glasses after the eye settles.

LASIK and vision correction

Laser vision correction reshapes the cornea so that glasses or contact lenses are needed less, or not at all, for short sight, long sight and astigmatism.

  • Who it suits: adults whose glasses power has been stable for about a year and whose corneas are healthy and thick enough.
  • Assessment: detailed corneal mapping and thickness measurement. Stop soft contact lenses for some days before the test, as advised.
  • The procedure: a few minutes per eye under numbing drops. Vision usually improves within a day or two.

Glaucoma

Glaucoma damages the optic nerve, usually because the pressure inside the eye is too high. Side vision is lost first and slowly, so many people notice nothing until late. Lost vision cannot be restored, but further loss can usually be prevented.

  • Who should be checked: everyone over 40, and earlier if a parent or sibling has glaucoma, or if you have diabetes, high myopia or use steroid medicines.
  • Diagnosis: eye pressure, an examination of the optic nerve, and visual field and imaging tests when needed.
  • Treatment: daily eye drops for most people; laser or surgery (such as trabeculectomy) when drops are not enough. Regular reviews are essential.

Retina

The retina is the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye. Its diseases often need prompt treatment.

  • Diabetic eye disease: diabetes can damage the retina's blood vessels without any symptoms. A dilated retina check every year is recommended for everyone with diabetes.
  • Macular degeneration: distortion or a blurred patch in the centre of vision, usually after 50. Some types are treated with injections into the eye.
  • Retinal tear and detachment: new floaters, flashes of light or a curtain over vision need an urgent examination the same day.
  • Tests: a dilated examination, retinal photographs and OCT scans. Dilating drops blur vision for a few hours: do not drive yourself home.

Eye check-ups

A complete check-up covers your vision, glasses power, eye pressure and a look at the front and back of the eye.

  • Children: before school, and any time a child squints, holds books close or complains of headaches.
  • Adults: every two years, and every year after 40 or with diabetes or high blood pressure.
  • Glasses: your prescription is given after the doctor's examination.
