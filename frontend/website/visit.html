Your visit

What to bring, what happens at the clinic, and how to prepare for surgery.

What to bringAt the clinicSurgery dayPayment and insurance

What to bring

  • Your current glasses and contact lens details.
  • Earlier eye reports, prescriptions and your patient number (UHID) if you have visited before.
  • A list of the medicines you take, including eye drops, and any allergies.
  • Someone to accompany you if your pupils may be dilated, or if you are elderly. Dilated pupils blur vision for a few hours.
  • Your ABHA number, if you have one, and insurance card or company letter if you are covered.

At the clinic

  1. Registration

    At reception you get a token number for the day.

  2. Vision tests

    Vision, glasses power and eye pressure with the optometrist.

  3. Doctor

    Examination, diagnosis, medicines and advice.

  4. Counselling

    Only if surgery is advised: procedure, package, cost and date.

  5. Billing

    Payment, prescription and your next appointment.

Surgery day

  • Follow the fasting instructions you are given. Take your usual blood pressure and other medicines unless the doctor told you otherwise.
  • Wear comfortable clothes, no make-up around the eyes, and come with an adult who can take you home.
  • Before surgery the team confirms your consent, marks the eye to be operated, checks your blood pressure and sugar, and dilates the pupil.
  • Most cataract operations take less than half an hour. You rest for a short while and go home the same day.
  • Afterwards: use the drops as prescribed, wear the shield at night, do not rub the eye, avoid water and dust in the eye, and come for your review the next day.

Payment and insurance

  • Cash, UPI and cards are accepted. You receive a bill for every payment.
  • For surgery covered by insurance, a TPA or your employer, tell the counsellor at the start. They will explain the documents and the approval process.
  • The counsellor explains the cost of a surgery package before you decide.

Go to an emergency department straight away if you have

  • sudden loss or dimming of vision in one or both eyes;
  • a sudden shower of floaters, flashes of light, or a curtain over part of your vision;
  • an injury to the eye, or a chemical splash (rinse with clean water for 15 minutes on the way);
  • a painful red eye with headache, nausea or haloes around lights.
